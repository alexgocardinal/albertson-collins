EXACT PROBLEM
1. Kahina Meslem and Eric Sopena, On the distinguishing number of cyclic
   tournaments: Towards the Albertson-Collins Conjecture, Discrete Applied
   Mathematics 266 (15 August 2019), 219-236.
   DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2018.10.021
   Full primary HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/1608.04866v4
   Version metadata: https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04866v4
   Submitted 17 August 2016; last revised 8 October 2018.
   Conjecture 2 (Introduction) is exactly the requested canonical high-half
   labeling: for all q>=1 and all cyclic T(2q+1;S), label {0,...,q} by 1,
   and the remaining vertices by 2. Distinguishing means only the identity
   automorphism preserves the labels. Thus the uploaded theorem addresses
   a prescribed subset, stronger than existence of any distinguishing
   2-labeling. Section 5 still asks to prove or disprove that conjecture.
   The paper gives sufficient conditions via rigid half subtournaments and
   proves the claim for every Paley tournament, among other classes.

2. Original note obtained directly from coauthor's institutional site:
   Michael O. Albertson and Karen L. Collins, A Note on Breaking the
   Symmetries of Tournaments, Congressus Numerantium 136 (1999), 129-131.
   https://kcollins.web.wesleyan.edu/publications/symnote-saks.pdf
   Volume/page metadata also listed on https://kcollins.web.wesleyan.edu/vita.htm
   Conjecture 2.4 uses the LOW half {0,...,t-1} for n=2t+1. This is equivalent
   to the modern HIGH-half formulation: the low half's complement is
   {t,...,2t}=I_n+t. A translate has a conjugate setwise stabilizer, and a
   subset and its complement have the same stabilizer. When matching the
   1999 statement explicitly, mention this equivalence rather than saying
   the two printed labelings are word-for-word identical. There is a likely
   final-variable typo in the original final endpoint, irrelevant here.

3. David Gluck, Trivial set-stabilizers in finite permutation groups,
   Canadian Journal of Mathematics 35(1) (1983), 59-67.
   DOI https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1983-005-2
   Primary publisher page:
   https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/trivial-setstabilizers-in-finite-permutation-groups/9667B85D9DDD0FE911C8382287B86167
   The odd-order regular-subset existence result is Corollary 1. It yields
   D(T)=2 for nontrivial cyclic tournaments but does not specify a half
   interval. Avoid claiming D(T)=2 itself as the new result.

JOURNAL OF GRAPH THEORY
Official current author guidelines read:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/10970118/homepage/forauthors.html
Graph structural results and related combinatorics fit its stated scope.
For a draft: abstract <=250 words; 4-7 keywords; full author name and
institutional affiliations; references alphabetized by first author's
surname. A LaTeX submission also requires a compiled PDF. Noncommercial
preprints such as arXiv are permitted. The guidelines require a data
availability statement and submitting-author ORCID. They expect supporting
data/scripts to be publicly archived when appropriate; an entirely
mathematical proof can accurately state that no datasets are used, keeping
finite exploratory checks separate. Do not invent funding, conflict-of-
interest, ORCID, or acknowledgments. The named journals should be treated
as alternative targets, not simultaneous submissions.
