ORIGINAL AUDIT -- 28 OCTOBER 2026
================================

Independent mathematical audit of the canonical-half theorem
Date: 28 October 2026 (client date)
Reviewer: independent model instance; this is not external human peer review.

Conclusion
----------
I find the universal group theorem mathematically correct, conditional only on the classical Burnside--Schur theorem in precisely its standard permutation-action form. The tournament corollary follows, and it is the prescribed-coloring conclusion, not merely existence of some two-color distinguishing coloring. This audit makes no novelty or priority determination and is not a Lean verification.

Objects and quantifiers
-----------------------
The claim concerns every odd integer n and every odd-order subgroup G of Sym(Z/nZ) containing the specific translation tau:x -> x+1. The set I_n uses the representatives 0,...,(n-1)/2. A finite set has a trivial setwise stabilizer when the only g with g(I_n)=I_n is the identity.

1. Base case and induction
--------------------------
n=1 is valid because Sym(1) is trivial. For n>1 odd, n>=3. Strong induction applies to every proper odd divisor a,b>1. A translate I+t is regular whenever I is: if g fixes I+t, then tau^(-t) g tau^t fixes I. A set and its complement have identical stabilizers. These two observations are essential and are correctly used for the low block intersections.

2. Structure of an invariant block system
-----------------------------------------
A nontrivial block B containing 0 is a subgroup of the additive cyclic group. For t in B, t is in both B and B+t; the block property and tau^t in G give B+t=B. This implies closure under addition and additive inverses (0 in B+t). Every other block is a translate of B, because tau is transitive. Thus for n=ab and 1<a,b<n, B consists of multiples of a, and the blocks are B_r={r+at:0<=t<b}, 0<=r<a. This requires no assumption that the whole group is cyclic, affine, or solvable. Odd n implies both factors odd.

3. Block intersection arithmetic
---------------------------------
For a=2u+1,b=2v+1, (ab-1)/2=av+u. Each representative r+at lies in the canonical interval if and only if t<=v for r<=u, and t<=v-1 for r>u. Consequently the high blocks have size v+1 exactly for r in I_a, and the others have size v. There is no wraparound ambiguity because 0<=r+at<ab in these coordinates.

4. First induction application
-------------------------------
The permutation group induced on blocks is an image of G and has odd order. tau induces r -> r+1. If g fixes I_n setwise, its induced block permutation preserves intersection cardinality, hence I_a. Induction makes this induced permutation the identity. Therefore g belongs to the setwise stabilizer of every B_r.

5. Second induction application
--------------------------------
The action of the setwise stabilizer G_{B_r} on B_r is an image of a subgroup of G, so has odd order. It contains tau^a, whose restriction is t -> t+1. For a high block the preserved subset is I_b. For a low block it is {0,...,v-1}, whose complement in Z_b is {v,...,2v}=I_b+v. Applying induction, translation conjugacy and complement equivalence forces the restriction of g to B_r to be the identity in either case. Thus g fixes all vertices.

6. Primitive reduction
-----------------------
The needed external theorem is: a primitive finite permutation group containing a regular cyclic subgroup is either 2-transitive or permutationally isomorphic to a subgroup of AGL_1(p) in prime degree p. The word permutationally is important. The proof uses this exact action-level conclusion. For n>=3, a 2-transitive group has order divisible by n(n-1), so has even order. The surviving case is prime degree with affine action. Parent review must verify the original theorem statement and exact conjecture from primary sources.

7. Alignment of affine coordinates
-----------------------------------
If f conjugates G to an affine group over F_p, then f tau f^(-1) has order p. An affine map x -> alpha*x+beta with alpha!=1 has a fixed point and is conjugate to multiplication by alpha, so its order divides p-1. Thus the image of tau is translation by c!=0. The relation f(j+1)=f(j)+c gives f(j)=f(0)+jc. Renormalizing the target coordinate by x -> (x-f(0))/c identifies it with the original residue coordinate. This step rules out the potential gap of applying interval arithmetic after an arbitrary relabeling.

8. The affine half-interval lemma
----------------------------------
For p>=5, put q=(p-1)/2 and m=q+1. In F_p, m=1/2, q=-1/2. Since m!=0, an affine stabilizer fixes the centroid c=q/2=-1/4. The usual sum identities give
sum_{j=0}^q (j-c)^2=m(m^2-1)/12=-1/32.
The denominators 2,3,4,12,32 are invertible for p>=5, and the moment is nonzero. Under a centered affine map it is multiplied by alpha^2. Invariance therefore gives alpha^2=1, hence alpha=1 or -1 in the field. alpha=1 and centroid fixation force beta=0. alpha=-1 and centroid fixation give x -> 2c-x, a nonidentity involution; Lagrange excludes it from an odd-order group. The exceptional characteristic p=3 is separately covered because |AGL_1(3)|=6 and an odd-order subgroup containing translations has order3. Alternatively, any affine permutation preserving a two-element set fixes its complement, and is identity or a transposition, so oddness immediately suffices.

9. Tournament application
--------------------------
Every nonidentity involution on a finite set has a two-cycle. An automorphism of a tournament cannot swap u,v, since it would map the unique arc u->v to v->u. Thus the finite automorphism group has no involution, and Cauchy's theorem gives odd order. A cyclic tournament has tau as an automorphism by its difference-set definition. Preserving named colors is exactly stabilizing I_n. The theorem proves that this coloring is distinguishing for every connector set and every odd order. It need not be a proper graph coloring; distinguishing colorings concern symmetry breaking.
